# Water and fixes: research notes (as of 11 Nov 2026)

## Water

Johor has little spare water. It used about 2,001 MLD in 2024, and Ranhill SAJ can treat about 2,352 MLD. In June 2026 the Deputy PM put the reserve margin at 14.4%, below the 15% national target. Singapore shares the Johor River, with rights to 250 mgd until 2061, and Johor buys back about 60 MLD of treated water. RM5bn of new works aim to end that by 2030.

On 31 Oct 2025 a burst sand mining pond on the Johor River cut water to about 800,000 people, showing how exposed the corridor is to one river.

In 2024, 13 Johor data centres asked for 29.1 MLD and were given 6.1 MLD (21%). The government projects their demand at 384 MLD by 2030, about a fifth of today's total state use. BIMB Research estimates actual use at about 18 MLD against 45 MLD booked.

Malaysian sources put a 100 MW site at about 4.2 MLD, or about 42,000 L/day per MW. MITI's recommended WUE of 2.2 m3/MWh works out to about 53,000 L/day per MW at full load (our arithmetic).

Since 1 Sep 2025 data centres have paid a separate water tariff of RM5.31/m3, against RM0.65 for low-use homes. Johor stopped approving Tier 1/2 sites, now approves only air-cooled Tier 3/4, and makes evaporative cooling requests wait until 2027. Two plants recycle sewage for data centres: Bridge (5 MLD at Ulu Tiram, planned 20) and ZData (4 MLD at Gelang Patah).

Batam residents went 3 to 6 months without piped water and blockaded Nongsa Digital Park on 5 Oct 2026. The data centres have their own reservoir pipeline.

## Energy fixes

- **In force:** CRESS (about 3.15 GW registered), cheaper GET green tariffs, RP4 tariffs that reflect cost, and take-or-pay rules. Data centres using under 85% of declared demand pay RM8.50/kW a month.
- **Being built:** 400 MW / 1.6 GWh of batteries (2027) and LSS6, which adds 2.65 GW of solar and 1.25 GW of batteries by end 2029.
- **Announced, no details yet:** a rule that data centres use at least 30% renewable power.
- **Planned:** a 1 GW Sarawak to Johor hydro cable. Feasibility study started Jan 2026, no completion date.
- **Under study:** nuclear, with a decision due by 2031.

## Conflicts

- **CRESS fee:** The Star reported 14 sen/kWh. The Energy Commission clarified 18 sen (14 + 4). Use 18.
- **LTMS-PIP Phase 2:** Malay Mail says a 30 MW minimum, TNB says up to 100 MW. Use TNB.
- **LSS6:** headlines say 2.5 GW; the lots add up to 2.65 GW.
- **Nuclear:** no official "nuclear by 2035" target found. The 2035 date in official statements is about retiring 7 GW of coal.
- **MD2030:** Malaysia Digital 2030, a digital action plan launched 29 Jun 2026.

## Takeaways

1. Johor has about 14% spare water, and data centres alone may need 384 MLD by 2030, roughly a fifth of what the state uses today.
2. Malaysia rations water to data centres, granting about a fifth of what they ask for and pushing them toward air cooling and recycled sewage.
3. Recycled water supplies under 10 MLD so far, so new dams and treatment plants have to carry most of the growth.
4. The energy fixes in force today change prices and contracts. The physical fixes (batteries, LSS6, the Sarawak cable, nuclear) arrive between 2027 and the 2030s.
5. In Batam, whatever officials said caused the shortages, residents blockaded the data centre park when their taps ran dry.
