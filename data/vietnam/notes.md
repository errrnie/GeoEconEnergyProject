# Vietnam data notes (built 2026-11-12)

## Method
Every row cites a page opened during the build. Primary sources first (EVN, AWS, FPT, NTT, CMC, government portal), then press (The Investor, VnExpress, Tuoi Tre, VietnamPlus, W.Media, Baxtel). High means a company, ministry or EVN states the figure; Medium is reputable press or one secondary source; Low is a reported plan or passing mention. MW is "IT" only when the source says so. MoU projects are Proposed with headline capex. Only the Tan Phu Trung rows have coordinates (Baxtel campus point); no sourced points were found for other parks. The fuel mix uses EVN's annual report for 2023 and 2024; no full-year 2022 fuel split was found and Ember's page could not be fetched. National capacity estimates disagree (JLL about 60MW for the two cities, a forum speaker 148MW, Savills 524.7MW), so all are logged with caveats. C4ADS diversion findings are allegations.

## Top 8 key moments
1. 2022-08-15: Decree 53 sets data localisation rules (effective 1 Oct 2022).
2. May to June 2023: northern blackouts cost about US$1.4bn (World Bank).
3. 2023-11-24: new Telecommunications Law passed, lifting foreign ownership caps on data centres (in force 1 Jul 2024, data centre clauses 1 Jan 2025).
4. 2024-12-05: Nvidia signs an AI research and data centre deal with the government.
5. 2025-04-15: revised PDP8 (Decision 768) lifts 2030 capacity to 183 to 236GW.
6. 2025-04-23: Viettel breaks ground on a 140MW campus, Vietnam's first above 100MW.
7. April 2026: first fully foreign hyperscale licences in Saigon Hi-Tech Park (Evolution 52MW, Sembcorp-BB Starmason 60MW).
8. 2026-06-19: AWS Local Zone Hanoi goes live, the first US hyperscaler infrastructure in Vietnam.

## Short history
Vietnam's data centre market grew up inside its big telecom and IT groups. Viettel, VNPT, FPT and CMC built small facilities in Hanoi's Hoa Lac and Ho Chi Minh City's Tan Thuan zone. Foreign operators such as NTT and STT GDC entered through local partners. The 2018 Cybersecurity Law and 2022's Decree 53 pushed user data to stay onshore. The 2023 Telecommunications Law changed the rules by allowing 100% foreign ownership of data centre and cloud services from 2025. Since then the pipeline has jumped, led by Viettel's 140MW build, the US$2.1bn SGI-HCM campus and Singaporean hyperscale licences. Delivery lags announcements, and STT GDC quit a 60MW project in 2026 over a short land lease.

## Energy security takeaways
In 2023 drought cut hydro, coal deliveries fell short and the north could not draw on southern surplus, leaving 900 GWh unmet. The government answered with a 591km 500kV line, built in six months, that doubled central-to-north transfer to 5,000MW. Coal then rose to 49.5% of supply in 2024, with hydro at 28.7% and gas 7.1% (EVN). LNG is just starting with the 1,624MW Nhon Trach 3 and 4 plants in 2025, which adds import-price exposure as gas grows. The 2024 DPPA decree lets large users buy renewables directly, but a new 100MW grid connection in the main clusters reportedly takes two to three years.

## 4 Gs draft
**Geology.** Vietnam runs on coal and water. In 2024 coal made about half of its electricity and hydro more than a quarter, so a dry year hits supply hard, as 2023 proved. LNG only arrived in 2025, and the revised power plan adds nuclear in the 2030s and large solar, wind and battery targets.

**Geography.** Demand and data centres sit at opposite ends of a long country. Hanoi's Hoa Lac and Ho Chi Minh City's Saigon Hi-Tech Park and Tan Phu Trung hold almost every project. The north-south grid is the weak link, and the 2024 500kV line was built to fix it. Singapore remains the regional anchor, and even AWS's Hanoi zone hangs off its Singapore region.

**Growth.** Electricity sales grew 9% in 2024. Data centre capacity is small, roughly 60 to 150MW operating depending on the source, against a government aim of 870MW by 2030. Build costs are about US$7.2m per MW versus US$9.6m in Malaysia. Most big projects are still licences or MoUs.

**Geopolitics.** Vietnam keeps top-level partnerships with both Washington and Beijing. It has US chips through FPT and Viettel, Gulf money through G42, Singaporean and Korean builders, and fresh Chinese offers. US tariffs swung from 20% to a 12.5% Section 301 rate in 2026, and a C4ADS report alleges Nvidia GPUs were diverted through Vietnam. Chip access is now part of its trade bargaining.

## Is Vietnam the next big host?
Not yet. Vietnam has the policy opening, low costs and a strong domestic builder in Viettel, but operating capacity is a fraction of Johor's (see malaysia/) and most foreign projects have not broken ground. Its coal-heavy, drought-exposed grid and slow connections point to a domestic and sovereign AI market first, with regional hosting later.
