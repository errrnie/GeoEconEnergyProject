Thailand runs its grid on gas that it is running out of. Gas made about 65% of the country's electricity in 2025, yet proved reserves in the Gulf of Thailand would last under five years at current output, and imported LNG already covers more than a quarter of gas supply. Every new data centre megawatt adds to a bill set by global LNG prices, with power bought from Lao dams and the Mae Moh lignite plant as partial backstops.

The data centres are going where industry already is. Nearly all live capacity sits in Bangkok and Chonburi, the Eastern Economic Corridor province that is home to Laem Chabang, the country's largest port, about 120 km from the capital. Getting power to those sites is the harder problem. EGAT is spending close to $1 billion to roughly double transfer capacity into the corridor, and industrial land there has risen 20 to 30% in two years as Chinese factories crowd in.

The economy grew only 2.4% in 2025 and the population is shrinking, yet investors have asked the grid for about 35 GW of data centre power, close to the country's whole peak load. Planners have pencilled in 8.8 GW. In September 2026 the government paused 49 projects under construction and 117 more awaiting approval while it writes new rules for the sector.

China is Thailand's most important trading partner, and Alibaba, Huawei, Tencent and TikTok are building here alongside AWS, Google and Microsoft. That mix worries Washington. A draft US rule to curb AI chip sales to Thailand was reportedly shelved in 2026, but prosecutors allege a Bangkok firm helped route about $2.5 billion of Nvidia servers toward China, and Thai goods now face a 12.5% US tariff while a trade deal waits to be signed.
