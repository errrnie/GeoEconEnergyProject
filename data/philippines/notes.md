# Philippines data notes (built 2026-11-12)

## Method
Every row cites a page opened during the build. Primary sources first (DOE, company releases), then press (BusinessWorld, Inquirer, Manila Bulletin, PNA, W.Media, DCD). High means a company or ministry states the figure; Medium is reputable press; Low is a plan or passing mention. MW is IT only where stated. The fuel mix uses DOE's 2024 Power Statistics Summary for 2022 to 2024; "renewables and hydro" sums geothermal, hydro, wind, solar and biomass. No coordinates were added because no opened source gave any. Capacity estimates disagree widely (JLL 73.6MW for Manila in 2025, BMI 140MW live in 2026, DICT anywhere from 180MW to "nearly 500MW"), so all are logged in energy.csv with caveats.

## Short history
The market grew out of the two telecom groups, PLDT and Globe, which built small sites around Manila, Cavite and Cebu for banks and call centres. In 2022 Globe put half of its data centres into a joint venture with Singapore's STT GDC and Ayala, and the same year Congress let foreigners fully own telecoms and renewable power. Singaporean newcomers such as Digital Edge and Digital Halo followed, mostly in Laguna and Rizal. AWS opened only a Manila Local Zone in 2023, while Huawei and Alibaba built local cloud zones. The step up came in 2025, when PLDT opened the 50MW VITRO Santa Rosa with NVIDIA GPUs, STT opened Fairview 1 and Equinix bought its way in. In 2026 Gulf investors pitched large projects and the government published an AI masterplan, but BMI counts only about 140MW live. High power prices are the brake, and SM Investments said in 2025 it would sell out of YCO Cloud for that reason.

## Energy security takeaways
Coal supplied 62.5% of power in 2024, up from 59.6% in 2022, with gas at 14.2% and all renewables plus hydro at 22.2% (DOE). The Malampaya field that feeds Batangas gas plants could run dry by 2027, so the country began importing LNG in April 2023, tying power costs to world gas prices. Luzon peak demand rose 11.7% in 2024 to 14,016MW, and in April 2024 forced outages at 19 plants pushed the grid into red alerts and rotating brownouts. World Bank data put residential power at about US$0.21/kWh, second only to Singapore in the region. The 10.2GW fourth green auction, mostly solar, helps from 2026 but does not give 24/7 firm supply. State Grid of China still owns 40% of the grid operator.

## 4 Gs draft
**Geology.** The Philippines has little fuel of its own. Its one gas field is running out, so gas now arrives as imported LNG. The real domestic assets are geothermal and hydro, which together made about 17% of power in 2024, while coal carries most of the load.

**Geography.** Data centres cluster around Manila and the provinces to the south and east (Laguna, Cavite, Rizal, Batangas), with a newer push north to Clark and Bataan. It has 21 submarine cables but ranks first on the 2025 WorldRiskIndex, which buyers weigh.

**Growth.** Power demand is growing about 8% a year in sales and over 11% at peak. Data centre capacity is small, roughly 140MW live, against a government target of 1.5GW of AI capacity by 2033. Most large projects, from PLDT's 100MW Cavite plan to DAMAC's 250MW, are still proposals.

**Geopolitics.** Like Thailand, the Philippines is a US treaty ally, but it has leaned in much harder, from the Luzon Economic Corridor to Pax Silica. Yet a Chinese cloud built the first full local region, a Chinese state firm co-owns the grid, and alliance status did not earn a better US tariff than Indonesia's 19%.

## Is the Philippines a serious host?
Not yet at scale. It has strong domestic demand, an open ownership regime and real political backing from Washington, but power costs, grid tightness and typhoon risk keep hyperscalers in Malaysia and Singapore for now. As a treaty ally it differs from its neighbours, Thailand included, by openly choosing a side through Pax Silica and the Clark zone, which could pull in US-aligned capital later while raising the stakes of any Chinese pressure.
