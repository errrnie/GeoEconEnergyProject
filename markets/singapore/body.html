Singapore's Rationed Hub

Singapore is still Southeast Asia's largest single data centre market on official and BMI counts, though Johor has drawn level on broker figures, and it is the region's network core. It is also out of land and runs almost entirely on imported gas, so it paused new data centres in 2019 and since 2022 has handed out capacity in small, competitive tranches. This page follows how the hub chose scarcity. The capacity it turned away crossed the causeway to Johor, which the Malaysia page picks up, and more slowly the strait to Batam.

DayOne

GDS's international arm became Singapore-headquartered DayOne, raised money from US, Chinese and Indonesian investors, and filed for a US listing in October 2026. It is the clearest case of a Chinese platform adapting by moving its address.

Billing versus shipping

Nvidia's FY2025 filing showed about 18% of revenue billed to Singapore customers but under 2% of revenue from products shipped there. Nvidia says customers centralise invoicing in Singapore, and it stopped reporting a Singapore line in FY2026, but the gap is why Washington watches the island closely.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Scarcity raised Singapore's value and pushed the megawatts next door

Sources: BMI via Technode, Jul 2026; Knight Frank Data Centre Atlas 2026; IMDA and EDB tender results, 2023 and 2026. Bars compare different measures and are for scale only.

Weak points

  • Almost all power comes from imported gas, Indonesian pipeline contracts run out by 2028, and Qatar's LNG stopped in April 2026.
  • A projected gas supply gap of about 3 million tonnes a year from 2028.
  • Its reputation as a trusted hub is being tested by the 2025 server fraud case and the Megaspeed reporting.

Leverage

  • Rationing lets the state pick winners and attach efficiency and green-fuel conditions.
  • It keeps the network core and the contracts, while Johor and Batam supply the megawatts.
  • A 6 GW low-carbon import target for 2035 and a 700 MW green park on Jurong Island give it a long-run path.
