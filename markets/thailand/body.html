Thailand's Compute Pause

In 2025 Thailand looked like the next Johor, with cheaper builds and new AWS and Google regions. In 2026 it chose to pause instead. In September 2026 it paused 166 data centre projects while it wrote new rules, and it is moving to charge data centres more for power. This page follows who came, what they asked for, and why Thailand chose to slow down.

2 · Why Thailand

The clouds came first, then the requests piled up

Spare power on paper

Thailand has 55.7 GW of contracted capacity against a record peak of 36.8 GW, a reserve margin of about 51% (IEEFA puts it near 55%). The real bottleneck is transmission into the Eastern Seaboard, where EGAT plans to roughly double transfer capacity from 600 to 1,150 MW.

Incentives and land

The Board of Investment offers up to eight years of tax holiday for high-efficiency data centres, and received more than 728 billion baht of data centre investment applications in 2025. Industrial land in Chonburi now tops out near 9.5 million baht per rai.

TikTok

Pledged US$8.8 billion for Thai data centres in February 2025, then won Board of Investment approval for 842 billion baht across three provinces in May 2026.

OBON and Siam AI

US prosecutors allege about US$2.5 billion of servers went through a Southeast Asian "Company-1"; Bloomberg's sources say it is Bangkok's OBON. OBON said in 2024 that it created Siam AI, Thailand's first Nvidia cloud partner, which was incorporated separately. OBON has not been charged. Siam AI says it imports GPUs for its own use and has separate headquarters from OBON.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Requests ran far ahead of the grid, so Thailand stopped the clock

Sources: Energy ministry via The Nation and Asia News Network, Sep 2026; draft Power Development Plan 2026 (high-growth case); The Nation and Asia News Network, Aug 2026, for pipeline and operating capacity. Bars are drawn to scale, so today's operating capacity is barely visible.

Weak points

  • Gas makes 54 to 60% of power (EPPO, 2024 to 2026), and new gas means imported LNG as Myanmar supply fades and Gulf of Thailand reserves run down.
  • The March 2026 Hormuz disruption showed how quickly LNG prices hit Thai power costs.
  • Eastern Seaboard reservoirs stood at 39 to 52% in June 2026, and operators do not disclose water use.
  • Three prime ministers since 2024 make policy continuity a real question for long-lived investments.

Leverage

  • A large power surplus on paper, so Thailand can choose which projects to connect.
  • A seven-tier licensing framework endorsed by Cabinet on 10 Nov 2026 lets it sort serious projects from speculative ones.
  • A separate data centre tariff category and a grid bond of 4.5 million baht per MW, both approved by the energy policy council on 15 Jul 2026, push costs back onto developers.
