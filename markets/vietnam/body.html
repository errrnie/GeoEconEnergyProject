Vietnam's Late Opening

Vietnam opened its data centre market to foreign owners only in 2025. Until then the market grew inside its own telecom and IT groups, led by the army-owned Viettel. Since the opening, Singaporean, Korean and Gulf builders have lined up, AWS has switched on in Hanoi and Viettel is building a 140 MW campus, but operating capacity is still a fraction of Johor's, on a coal and hydro grid that ran short in the north in 2023.

2 · Why Vietnam

Cheap to build, newly open, with a home champion

Select a card with a link to see the numbers behind it.

Opened in 2025

The 2023 Telecommunications Law removed the foreign ownership cap on data centre and cloud services from January 2025. In April 2026 Ho Chi Minh City licensed its first fully foreign hyperscale projects, Singapore-led Evolution (52 MW) and Sembcorp's Starmason (60 MW).

A state champion leads

Viettel, owned by the defence ministry, opened a 30 MW campus at Hoa Lac in 2024 and broke ground on a 140 MW one in 2025, Vietnam's first above 100 MW. FPT and CMC follow, with Nvidia and Samsung as partners.

US clouds, one step at a time

AWS's Hanoi Local Zone, live since June 2026, is the first US hyperscaler infrastructure in Vietnam. Google was reported in 2024 to be weighing a hyperscale site near Ho Chi Minh City. US chips arrive mainly through FPT and Viettel.

Chinese clouds are circling

Huawei and Alibaba were reported in 2024 to be weighing Vietnamese data centres, and neither has announced one. Vietnam keeps top-level partnerships with both Washington and Beijing, and Gulf capital arrived in 2025 through a G42-led US$2 billion proposal in Ho Chi Minh City.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Plans run ahead of delivery, and nobody agrees on the base

Sources: Savills via Vietnamese press; government aim and the 148 MW figure as cited at an August 2026 forum (VnExpress); Cushman & Wakefield, April 2026. The figures use different definitions and are shown for scale.

Weak points

  • A dry year cuts hydro, and in 2023 the north ran short for weeks; most data centres sit at the two ends of the long north-south grid.
  • A large grid connection reportedly takes two to three years in the main clusters.
  • Short land leases deter hyperscale: STT GDC sold out of a 60 MW Ho Chi Minh City project in 2026 because its lease ran only to 2041.
  • US tariffs have swung three times since 2025, and a 2026 report alleges GPUs were diverted through Vietnam.

Leverage

  • The lowest build cost in the region and a large, fast-growing home market with data localisation rules.
  • Domestic builders with state backing, led by Viettel, who do not wait for foreign tenants.
  • Partnerships with the US, China and the Gulf at once, and chip access now part of its trade talks with Washington.
