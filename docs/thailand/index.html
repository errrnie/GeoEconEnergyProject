Thailand's Compute Pause

Thailand drew some of the region's largest cloud pledges after Malaysia, then hit the brakes. In September 2026 it paused 166 data centre projects while it wrote new rules, and it now plans to charge data centres more for power. This page follows who came, what they asked for, and why Thailand chose to slow down.

Spare power on paper

Thailand has 55.7 GW of contracted capacity against a record peak of 36.8 GW, a reserve margin near 55%. The real bottleneck is transmission into the Eastern Seaboard, where EGAT plans to roughly double transfer capacity from 600 to 1,150 MW.

Incentives and land

The Board of Investment offers up to eight years of tax holiday for high-efficiency data centres, and received more than 728 billion baht of data centre investment applications in 2025. Industrial land in Chonburi now tops out near 9.5 million baht per rai.

6 · Chip access and security

Thailand sits in the same middle tier, under the same suspicion

In January 2025 the US sorted the world into three tiers for advanced AI chips, and Thailand landed in the middle tier with Malaysia. The rule was scrapped four months later, but a US draft aimed specifically at Malaysia and Thailand followed in July 2025, and the worry behind it, chips leaking to China, now runs through Bangkok too.

Thailand's position today

No US licence or cap applies to Thailand as a country. The July 2025 draft rule for Malaysia and Thailand was reportedly shelved in August 2026. Thailand has tightened its own checks instead: from May 2026 the Board of Investment and Customs cut incentives for "pass-through" assembly and began checking all electronics trade, and a joint watch list with US customs grew to 67 items in June. As in Malaysia, a Chinese-parented buyer needs a US licence for top chips wherever it operates.

The OBON and Siam AI case

In March 2026 US prosecutors charged a Supermicro co-founder and two others, alleging about US$2.5 billion of Nvidia servers moved through a Southeast Asian "Company-1" in 2024 and 2025. Bloomberg's sources name Company-1 as OBON, a Bangkok firm that created Siam AI, Thailand's first Nvidia cloud partner. OBON has not been charged, Alibaba denies any link, and Siam AI says it is independent and has not exported to China.

7 · Energy security

Thailand already lives on imported LNG

Gas makes most of Thailand's power, and the gas is running short at home. Gulf of Thailand output has slipped since the Erawan field changed hands, Myanmar's pipeline supply is fading, and proved reserves would last under five years at current output. New data centre load therefore lands on an LNG import bill that is already large.

Who pays

Thailand has chosen to bill data centres directly. In July 2026 the national energy policy council approved a separate tariff category for data centres, with local media reporting a proposed rate of 5 to 6 baht per kWh against an average tariff of about 3.95, and a grid-access bond of 4.5 million baht per MW has been proposed. EGAT still carries about 31 billion baht of fuel costs it was not allowed to pass on to consumers.

Compared with Malaysia

Malaysia's exposure today is mostly imported coal, and its data centre boom is pushing it toward LNG from a small base. Thailand already imports about five times as much LNG, so data centres add to a bill that is large and swings with world prices. Malaysia promised 8.3 GW and kept building; Thailand paused 166 projects and raised the price of power. Thailand is trading speed for protection of households.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Requests ran far ahead of the grid, so Thailand stopped the clock

Sources: Energy ministry via The Nation and Asia News Network, Sep 2026; draft Power Development Plan 2026; Cushman & Wakefield and EGAT for pipeline and current load. Bars are drawn to scale, so today's load is barely visible.

Weak points

  • About 58% of power comes from gas, and new gas means imported LNG as Gulf of Thailand and Myanmar supply decline.
  • The March 2026 Hormuz disruption showed how quickly LNG prices hit Thai power costs.
  • Eastern Seaboard reservoirs stood at 39 to 52% in June 2026, and operators do not disclose water use.
  • Three prime ministers since 2024 make policy continuity a real question for long-lived investments.

Leverage

  • A large power surplus on paper, so Thailand can choose which projects to connect.
  • A seven-tier licensing scheme endorsed by Cabinet on 10 Nov 2026 lets it sort serious projects from speculative ones.
  • A separate data centre tariff and a proposed grid bond of 4.5 million baht per MW push costs back onto developers.
