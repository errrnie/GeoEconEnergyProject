Vietnam's Late Opening

Vietnam opened its data centre market to foreign owners only in 2025. Until then the market grew inside its own telecom and IT groups, led by the army-owned Viettel. Since the opening, Singaporean, Korean and Gulf builders have lined up, AWS has switched on in Hanoi and Viettel is building a 140 MW campus, but operating capacity is still a fraction of Johor's, on a coal and hydro grid that ran short in the north in 2023.

2 · Why Vietnam

Cheap to build, newly open, with a home champion

Select a card with a link to see the numbers behind it.

Opened in 2025

The 2023 Telecommunications Law removed the foreign ownership cap on data centre and cloud services from January 2025. In April 2026 Ho Chi Minh City licensed its first fully foreign hyperscale projects, Singapore-led Evolution (52 MW) and Sembcorp's Starmason (60 MW).

A state champion leads

Viettel, owned by the defence ministry, opened a 30 MW campus at Hoa Lac in 2024 and broke ground on a 140 MW one in 2025, Vietnam's first above 100 MW. FPT and CMC follow, with Nvidia and Samsung as partners.

US clouds, one step at a time

AWS's Hanoi Local Zone, live since June 2026, is the first US hyperscaler infrastructure in Vietnam. Google was reported in 2024 to be weighing a hyperscale site near Ho Chi Minh City. US chips arrive mainly through FPT and Viettel.

Chinese clouds are circling

Huawei and Alibaba were reported in 2024 to be weighing Vietnamese data centres, and neither has announced one. Vietnam keeps top-level partnerships with both Washington and Beijing, and Gulf capital arrived in 2025 through a G42-led US$2 billion proposal in Ho Chi Minh City.

6 · Chip access and security

Already licensed before the AI rules, and named in a diversion report

Vietnam sat in the middle tier of the January 2025 US chip rule, like its neighbours. Unlike them, it already needed a US licence for advanced AI chips under rules issued in October 2023 (in force 17 November 2023), because the US lists it in a group of countries with arms-related controls. Those licences are usually approved, and the rule's rescission in May 2025 did not change that.

US chips through local champions

Nvidia signed an agreement with the government in December 2024 to set up an AI research centre and an AI data centre, and FPT readied an AI factory with thousands of H100s the same month. Viettel put Vietnam's first Nvidia DGX B200 into service in February 2026. In the October 2025 trade framework, Vietnam asked to be taken off the US control lists that make these licences necessary.

The diversion allegation

A September 2026 C4ADS report alleged that 50 shipments of restricted Nvidia GPUs, worth about US$13.4 million, were routed through Vietnam, India and Malaysia to Hong Kong and China in 2023 to 2025. These are allegations, and no Vietnamese firm was named. In August 2026, Chinese speakers at a forum in Hanoi offered to build AI factories with Chinese GPUs and capital; no government commitment was reported.

7 · Energy security

Vietnam's limit is the wire to the north, more than the fuel

Vietnam runs on coal and water. In 2024 coal made about half its electricity and hydro more than a quarter, so a dry year hits hard. In May and June 2023 drought cut hydro, coal deliveries fell short and the north could not draw on surplus power in the south, leaving 900 GWh unmet. The government answered with a 591 km, 500 kV line built in six months. Data centres sit at both ends of that line, in Hanoi and Ho Chi Minh City.

Demand above today's roughly 73 MW is assumed met by imported LNG, the fuel the revised power plan grows fastest (22.5 GW of LNG plants by 2030, with coal held at about 31 GW), at a 7.3 GJ/MWh heat rate. In 2024 coal met most new demand, so the real mix may be dirtier. Low: Cushman & Wakefield's 73 MW operating plus 137 MW pipeline to 2030. Mid: the government's 870 MW aim. High: Savills' 950 MW forecast. Even the high case is about 2% of 2024 generation.

Small load, weak links

Electricity sales grew 9% in 2024, about 23 TWh in one year. Every scenario above adds less than a third of that by 2030. The risk is where the load lands and when: a dry season, a coal shortfall or a congested line, plus a reported two to three years to connect 100 MW in the main clusters. A 2024 decree lets large users buy renewable power directly, and the first LNG plants, 1,624 MW at Nhon Trach, came online in 2025.

Compared with its neighbours

Malaysia's high case needs about 7 Mt of extra LNG a year, Thailand's about 9 Mt. Vietnam's needs under 1 Mt because its data centre market is still small. Indonesia burns its own coal. If Vietnam's pipeline grows the way Johor's did, it will add LNG import exposure just as the country starts importing LNG for the first time.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Plans run ahead of delivery, and nobody agrees on the base

Sources: Savills via Vietnamese press; government aim and the 148 MW figure as cited at an August 2026 forum (VnExpress); Cushman & Wakefield, April 2026. The figures use different definitions and are shown for scale.

Weak points

  • A dry year cuts hydro, and in 2023 the north ran short for weeks; most data centres sit at the two ends of the long north-south grid.
  • A large grid connection reportedly takes two to three years in the main clusters.
  • Short land leases deter hyperscale: STT GDC sold out of a 60 MW Ho Chi Minh City project in 2026 because its lease ran only to 2041.
  • US tariffs have swung three times since 2025, and a 2026 report alleges GPUs were diverted through Vietnam.

Leverage

  • The lowest build cost in the region and a large, fast-growing home market with data localisation rules.
  • Domestic builders with state backing, led by Viettel, who do not wait for foreign tenants.
  • Partnerships with the US, China and the Gulf at once, and chip access now part of its trade talks with Washington.
