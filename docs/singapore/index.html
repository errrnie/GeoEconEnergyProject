Singapore's Rationed Hub

Singapore is still Southeast Asia's largest data centre market and its network core. It is also out of land and runs almost entirely on imported gas, so since 2019 it has handed out new capacity in small, competitive tranches. The big server halls went next door to Johor and Batam. This page follows how the hub chose scarcity and what that did to the region.

DayOne

GDS's international arm became Singapore-headquartered DayOne, raised money from US, Chinese and Indonesian investors, and filed for a US listing in October 2026. It is the clearest case of a Chinese platform adapting by moving its address.

Billing versus shipping

Nvidia's FY2025 filing showed about 18% of revenue billed to Singapore customers but under 2% of shipments going there. Most of that reflects regional headquarters, but it is why Washington watches the island closely.

6 · Chip access and security

The trusted hub is where the chip money is booked

Singapore was in the middle tier of the January 2025 US chip rule, alongside Malaysia and Thailand. It needs no US country licence today, but as the place where much of the region's chip trade is booked, it sits at the centre of Washington's diversion worries.

Singapore's position today

Singapore is in US Country Group A:6, so it needs no country licence for advanced chips. As elsewhere, supplying a Chinese-parented buyer requires a US licence wherever it operates. In February 2025 Singapore charged three men in a US$390 million server fraud case reportedly linked to Nvidia chips.

Megaspeed

Megaspeed is registered in Singapore, with its largest operation in Malaysia. Since October 2025 the New York Times and Bloomberg have reported US and Singapore investigations into whether it diverted Nvidia chips to China, and in November 2026 reports alleged larger flows through Singapore and Malaysia. These are allegations. Megaspeed denies any diversion, and nobody has been charged.

7 · Energy security

An island that imports all of its power fuel

Singapore has no oil, no gas, no rivers for hydro and little room for solar. Gas makes 93% of its electricity, about half by pipeline from Indonesia and Malaysia and half as LNG. The Indonesian pipeline contracts run out by 2028, and Qatar's cargoes stopped in April 2026 when war hit the Gulf. Rationing data centres is partly an energy security decision.

The import route out

Singapore plans to import up to 6 GW of low-carbon power by 2035, has conditionally approved 3.4 GW from Indonesia and 1.75 GW from the Australia-Asia PowerLink, and began buying small volumes from Laos via Thailand and Malaysia in 2022 and from Malaysia's ENEGEM exchange in 2024. Most of these projects are still conditional.

Compared with its neighbours

Singapore's whole grid is a gas import problem, so it guards each new megawatt. Malaysia and Thailand still have some domestic fuel and much more land, which is why the region's growth went to them. Singapore exports the hardest part of the energy problem to its neighbours and keeps the network, the contracts and the headquarters.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Weak points

  • Almost all power comes from imported gas, Indonesian pipeline contracts run out by 2028, and Qatar's LNG stopped in April 2026.
  • A projected gas supply gap of about 3 million tonnes a year from 2028.
  • Its neutral-hub reputation is being tested by the 2025 server fraud case and the Megaspeed reporting.

Leverage

  • Rationing lets the state pick winners and attach efficiency and green-fuel conditions.
  • It keeps the network core and the contracts, while Johor and Batam supply the megawatts.
  • A 6 GW low-carbon import target for 2035 and a 700 MW green park on Jurong Island give it a long-run path.
