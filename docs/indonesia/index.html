Indonesia's Two Fronts

Indonesia is two data centre markets. Greater Jakarta serves the region's largest population, with US and Chinese cloud regions and rules that keep public data at home, and runs on a coal-heavy grid. Batam, 20 km from Singapore, became Singapore's other overflow after Johor, on a small island grid where water is now the tighter limit. This page follows both.

Batam, Singapore's other overflow

Nongsa Digital Park opened in 2018, 20 km from Singapore with its own utility and new subsea cables. In April 2026 DayOne signed a 450 MW power deal with PLN Batam, and Firmus and Nvidia followed with plans for a 360 MW AI factory.

Slow to connect

A 100 MW grid connection takes about 36 months, against about 12 under Malaysia's Green Lane. Java's reserve margin is shrinking, and Java had rolling blackouts in June 2026.

DayOne in Batam

The former international arm of China's GDS, now headquartered in Singapore, signed a 450 MW power deal with PLN Batam in April 2026. That one contract is more than half of the island grid's 844 MW capability.

Chinese capital beyond data centres

Chinese firms control about three quarters of Indonesia's nickel refining, and CATL broke ground in 2025 on a US$6 billion battery chain ending in a cell plant in Karawang, the same corridor as Jakarta's data centres.

6 · Chip access and security

Never singled out on chips, but not out of the picture

Indonesia sat in the middle tier of the January 2025 US chip rule with its neighbours. Unlike Malaysia and Thailand, it was never the target of a country-specific US draft, and it needs no US country licence for AI chips today.

Indonesia's position today

Indonesia is outside the US country groups that need licences for advanced chips, and no Indonesian enforcement case on AI chips was found. As everywhere, supplying a Chinese-parented buyer requires a US licence. Indonesia joined BRICS as a full member in January 2025, and its February 2026 trade deal with Washington is signed but not in force; Indonesian goods now pay a 10% US duty.

Megaspeed's Indonesian unit

Bloomberg's analysis of customs records says Singapore-based Megaspeed's affiliates in Malaysia and Indonesia imported at least US$4.6 billion of Nvidia hardware in 2023 to 2025, without splitting the figure between the two. US and Singapore investigations are reported as ongoing. These are allegations. Megaspeed denies any diversion, and no Indonesian case has been reported.

7 · Energy security

On Java, the data centre bet is a coal bet

Indonesia is rich in coal and gas, and its data centres get the coal. Coal made about two thirds of national power in 2025, above the government's own target, and PLN buys it at a capped price. Almost all data centre load sits on the Java-Bali grid, which runs about 60% on coal. Batam is the exception, a separate gas-fired island grid now being fed with Natuna gas that once went to Singapore.

Cheap power, slow connections

Capped coal keeps power cheap, about 6.3 US cents per kWh by one broker estimate. The constraint is time: about 36 months for a large connection, and a planning reserve on Java that falls toward 33% by 2027. The national power plan adds no fossil capacity on Java after 2029, and of US$21.4 billion pledged under the clean-energy transition partnership, only about US$3.1 billion has been approved.

Compared with its neighbours

Malaysia and Thailand will meet extra data centre load mostly with imported LNG. Indonesia meets it with its own coal, and in Batam with gas held back from export. That makes Indonesia the most fuel-secure of the region's hosts and the most carbon-heavy, and its bottleneck is connecting to the grid rather than paying for fuel.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Requests run far ahead of the grid, and Batam runs short of water

Sources: PLN and the energy ministry via Indonesian press, 2025 to 2026; IDPRO industry association; BP Batam and local water data. The forecasts measure different things and are shown for scale.

Weak points

  • On Java the marginal fuel is coal, which makes data centres here the most carbon-heavy in the region and hard to sell to buyers with clean-power targets.
  • Grid connections take about three years, and Java's spare capacity is shrinking.
  • In Batam one 450 MW deal takes more than half of the island grid, and one reservoir supplies 80% of the island's water.
  • The national data centre was crippled by ransomware in June 2024.

Leverage

  • The largest home market in Southeast Asia, with rules that keep public and financial data onshore.
  • Its own coal and gas, so it is the least exposed of the region's hosts to imported fuel prices.
  • A new sovereign fund, Danantara, says it is pushing more than US$1.7 billion into data centres.
