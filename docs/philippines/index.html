The Philippines' Allied Bet

The Philippines is the US treaty ally among Southeast Asia's data centre hosts, and since 2024 it has openly chosen Washington's side on chips and AI. Its market grew out of the PLDT and Globe telecom groups around Manila and opened to full foreign ownership in 2022. Yet a Chinese cloud built the first full local region, a Chinese state firm co-owns the grid, and power costs close to Singapore's keep the big hyperscalers in Malaysia for now.

2 · Why the Philippines

Open to foreign owners, backed by Washington, held back by power

Select a card with a link to see the numbers behind it.

Opened in 2022

Congress let foreigners fully own telecoms in March 2022, and renewable power followed in November. Globe put its data centres into a joint venture with Singapore's STT GDC and Ayala, and Singaporean builders such as Digital Edge and Digital Halo followed.

The ally's corridor

The Luzon Economic Corridor (2024), the Pax Silica declaration (2026) and a proposed US-backed chip and AI hub at New Clark City point US-aligned money north of Manila, toward Clark and Bataan. The state developer says that hub will host factories, not large data centres.

US firms, mostly through partners

PLDT's 50 MW VITRO Santa Rosa, formally opened in April 2025, hosts the country's first Nvidia GPU servers. Equinix bought three Manila data centres in 2025. AWS runs a Local Zone, and a full AWS region was discussed in July 2026 but not launched.

Chinese clouds and a Chinese grid partner

Huawei Cloud runs a three-zone region and Alibaba Cloud a Manila zone. State Grid of China owns 40% of NGCP, the grid operator, and the sovereign fund Maharlika has said it wants that stake if it becomes available.

6 · Chip access and security

A treaty ally with no special chip treatment

The Philippines is a US treaty ally, yet the January 2025 US chip rule put it in the capped middle tier with its neighbours, not in the top tier with close allies. The rule was rescinded in May 2025 before compliance began, and today the Philippines needs no US country licence for advanced AI chips.

Choosing a side in public

In April 2024 the US, Japan and the Philippines launched the Luzon Economic Corridor linking Subic, Clark, Manila and Batangas. In April 2026 the Philippines signed the US-led Pax Silica declaration on chip and AI supply chains, and a US-backed chip and AI hub has been proposed for New Clark City, on former US base land, though the state developer says it will host chip and electronics plants rather than large data centres. No other host on this site has aligned this openly.

Alliance did not buy a better deal

Since July 2026 Philippine goods pay a 12.5% US Section 301 tariff, the same as Singapore, Thailand and Vietnam and above Malaysia and Indonesia at 10%. On chips, as everywhere, supplying a China-parented buyer needs a US licence. No Philippine chip diversion case was found.

7 · Energy security

Coal carries the load, and new gas has to be imported

The Philippines has little fuel of its own. Coal made 62.5% of power in 2024, up from 59.6% in 2022, and the Malampaya gas field that feeds the Batangas plants was expected to run dry around 2027, so the country began importing LNG in April 2023. New Malampaya wells began flowing in November 2026 and should extend output to about 2034, but LNG imports keep rising. Luzon, where almost every data centre sits, saw peak demand rise 11.7% in 2024, and in April that year forced outages at 19 plants put the grid on red alert with rotating brownouts.

Expensive power is the brake

The World Bank puts residential power at about US$0.21 per kWh, against US$0.05 in Malaysia and US$0.09 in Indonesia. Meralco says large users can get about 11 US cents, comparable to Thailand and Malaysia, but SM Investments said in 2025 it would sell its stake in YCO Cloud, calling power very expensive. The Department of Energy said Philippine rates were the highest in ASEAN in June 2026. A 10.2 GW green energy auction, mostly solar and due online between 2026 and 2029, helps but does not give round-the-clock supply.

Compared with its neighbours

The mid case alone would need about as much extra LNG as the Philippines was expected to import in all of 2024. Malaysia and Thailand already import far more LNG, and Indonesia burns its own coal. The Philippines is closest to Vietnam, a small market just starting on imported LNG, but with power prices near Singapore's.

8 · Leverage and weak points

Targets run far ahead of what is built

Sources: DICT via Philippine press, Feb 2026; AI+ Infrastructure Masterplan 2026-2033 (W.Media); Meralco, May 2024; BMI via TNGlobal, Sep 2026. The figures measure different things and are shown for scale.

Weak points

  • Power is among the most expensive in the region, and a major local investor cited it when it said it would exit a data centre firm in 2025.
  • The Luzon grid is tight, with red alerts and brownouts in April 2024, and domestic gas is in decline.
  • The country ranks first on the 2025 WorldRiskIndex for disaster risk, which buyers weigh.
  • A Chinese state firm co-owns the grid operator while the government courts US-aligned AI investment.

Leverage

  • Open ownership rules since 2022 and a large home market served by its own telecom groups.
  • Clear political backing from Washington and Tokyo, from the Luzon corridor to Pax Silica.
  • A 10.2 GW green energy auction and geothermal and hydro of its own to build on.
